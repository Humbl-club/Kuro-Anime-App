Five different design languages.

Try each switch. Scroll inside the preview to see it with Home.

Noir

Cinematic · floating bottom control

Open full preview ↗
Why this direction

Dark graphite Home; a small floating switch stays in thumb reach. The top is reserved for your titles.

Tradeoff: the dock overlays a small part of the feed. A native version needs safe-area and keyboard handling.

Poster

Selected direction · polished artwork typography

Open full preview ↗
Why this direction

Selected by the owner. Artwork-filled typography, quieter registration line, readable inactive labels and aligned card/button corners.

Tradeoff: the lettering needs catalogue-wide contrast checks; the solid ink background keeps missing artwork readable.

Glass

Light · control on the artwork plane

Open full preview ↗
Why this direction

A translucent selector rests above the first artwork, joining navigation and imagery. Rounded cards and a cool background create a softer visual language.

Tradeoff: it covers some artwork, so placement and blur must be checked on-device. Direct labels stay visible.

Edgewise

Publication · vertical edge tabs

Open full preview ↗
Why this direction

Anime and Manga become tabs along the page edge. A reserved margin and single-column secondary list make this feel like a personal publication.

Tradeoff: it sacrifices some content width. The vertical text and 44px-wide targets need device review.

Keys

Instrument · tactile controls beside your updates

Open full preview ↗
Why this direction

Two compact keys share the release-count row. A small press and shallow key surfaces make switching deliberate, while removing a separate selector row.

Tradeoff: the release-count column is tighter on small phones. These are proposed visual key surfaces, not simulated physical haptics.
