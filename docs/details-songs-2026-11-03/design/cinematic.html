Kuro · Details, songs & platform · direction 03 of the set · 2026-11-03

Cinematic.
The title page as a poster that unfolds.

The page opens on the artwork, larger than today: a poster window with Home's season tabs seated on its lower edge. Under it comes the material band with the title, a facts strip in Home's readout language, and a perforated ticket for the next episode. Scrolling unfolds the film: the story, a timeline of episodes with stills, songs as a shelf of record sleeves with 30-second previews, the cast with their voices, and more like this.

It stays inside Home's grammar. The page is kuroPaper, and colour only appears on or under the title's own art (amendment 2). Radii are Home's measured literals, keys are physical, and there are no black filter pills or letter-spaced serif caps. Serif only where the prototype already uses it.

The data is real. Titles, airing times, characters, voice actors, studios, episode stills and song credits were read on 2026-11-03 from the public AniList and AnimeThemes APIs. Apple artwork was found by real iTunes Search queries, and the match and no-match states below are what those queries returned. Personal progress, stats and chapter numbers are sample values and are labelled as such.

Match rule (real iTunes Search results, 2026-11-03)

Normalise the title and the artist: case, width, punctuation, "feat.", and version suffixes such as "(TV size)" or "- ANIME edit". Accept only when both are equal, the result is a song, and it is not a live take. Prefer the single or EP. Cache the decision per song, and never show a match that wasn't made.

The direction in five lines

  • Poster first. A 428 pt artwork window (today's hero is 150 pt), Home's season tabs on its lower edge, then the material band: title, facts strip and status.
  • Ticket for time. The next release is a perforated ticket in the title's wash colour. A confirmed instant gets a clock time and a bell. An estimate gets a day, the word "expected", an "Estimate" label and no bell.
  • Film strip for episodes. One timeline rail: watched runs collapse, the next episode is the ticket again, and stills come from the source when it has them, with a branded still tile when it doesn't.
  • Records for songs. Sleeves grouped by cour. ▶ previews in place, and the record slides out while it plays. Only real matches get artwork. Spoilers stay wrapped.
  • People are pages. Character ↔ voice actor in both directions, studios as leaves, every rail with a visible edge peek.

Discover All and small finishes (item 13)

  • Edge peek everywhere. Each horizontal rail sets its trailing inset to −gutter, so the next card is cut at the screen edge (about 18–30 pt of it shows). This covers Discover Trending, the entity "Appears in" and "Voiced in" rails, and the detail "More like this" and cast rails.
  • Discover All ≤ about 2,000 pt. Lead decision: "Manga new to you" becomes one rail on All (a 106 × 150 rail plus its heading, about 290 pt instead of the feature + trio at about 520). "Essential manga" moves into the fold. The Anime and Manga segments are unchanged.
  • VoiceOver order, correct by construction. Each card is .accessibilityElement(children: .combine) with the Plan / +1 / voice chip as a separate element after it. The hero reads kicker → title → status → facts (one element: "2026, TV, 12 episodes, OLM, score 8.5") → ticket (one element plus the bell button). Open: not device-checked tonight.

Open items found while designing

  1. Episode stills attached to the wrong season (item 3, data risk). AniList's streamingEpisodes for JJK Season 2 (145064) are Season 1's ("Episode 1 – Ryomen Sukuna", 24 entries for a 23-episode season). The Apothecary Season 1 record (161645) carries Season 2's list (Episode 25–48). The app imports this field, so some of the 49k titled episodes may sit on the wrong season. The timeline needs a guard before it shows stills: entry count ≤ episodes, and the numbering matches the season or is offset by the prequel's episode count.
  2. No stills for current seasons. Every Fall 2026 TV title in AniList's top 40 has an empty streamingEpisodes. The branded still tile is therefore the common case for airing shows, not an edge case.
  3. Song alias rule. "Ao no Sumika" vs "Where Our Blue Is". Should the matcher accept the same exact artist plus a Japanese-store native title (青のすみか / キタニタツヤ)? Until someone decides, it stays a no match.
  4. Missing artists. AnimeThemes lists no artist for Mob Psycho 100's "Refrain Boy" (ALL OFF). Rows say "Artist not listed" and get no Apple match.
  5. "Your year" semantics. LibraryEntry has only createdAt/updatedAt, so the card says what it counts. A real finish date would need a schema change, which is not proposed.
  6. VoiceOver order: correct by construction (above), not device-checked.
  7. Import button case: keep the upper-case string, or set the same words in sentence case (shown). This is a copy-rule call for the lead.
