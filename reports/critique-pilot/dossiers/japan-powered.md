# Japan Powered — site dossier (Round 0 extension, 2026-08-05)

**Status: ACTIVE** (weekly Sunday cadence verified: 2026-08-02, -07-26, -07-19, -07-12,
-07-05, -06-28; latest post "Akane-banashi Brings Cultural Enrichment to Anime Watchers",
2026-08-02). Owner-proposed, NOT BLESSED.

- **Name:** Japan Powered ("Explore the intersection between anime and culture.")
- **URL:** https://www.japanpowered.com/
- **Anime/manga/both:** Both, anime-first. No dedicated manga category slug, but manga
  essays live inside the anime/otaku categories (site began as a library Manga Club project;
  recent manga-adjacent pieces exist, e.g. "This Monster Wants to Eat Me").
- **Language:** English
- **Named critics:** Yes — single author: **Chris Kincaid**, real-name, a working librarian
  with a computer-animation degree and published books on Japanese folklore (site "My Newest
  Books" widget; "Come and Sleep: The Folklore of the Japanese Fox"). Writing under his own
  name since ~2011 ("Lessons from 10 Years of Blogging" ≈2021). One stable voice.
- **Review depth:** Read in full: "Golden Time: How Insecurity Splits Oneself"
  (2024-10-27) — **2,452 words**, spoiler-complete synopsis + a sustained psychological
  argument (identity integration; critique of anime's amnesia trope) ending in explicit
  craft verdicts ("The characters behave in realistic, believable ways"; "Golden Time's
  largest weakness is how nice all the characters are"). The about page states most
  editorials carry APA-style reference lists drawn from academic databases — unusual rigor
  for the space. It ARGUES, but often toward cultural/psychological meaning rather than
  craft scoring.
- **Axis mapping (Kuro axes):** story/characters — strong (thesis-driven character and
  narrative analysis); legacy — strong (Evangelion retrospectives, "Top 100 Anime",
  "Commodification and the End of Anime", which engages a 2026 Ryota Fujitsu interview —
  converges with the slate's JP anchor); pacing — occasional; visuals/craft — weak (he is
  explicit that he's not a sakuga-style technical writer). Content notes: plausible
  secondary source (covers dark material head-on, e.g. suicide-adjacent themes in "This
  Monster Wants to Eat Me").
- **Archive size estimate:** COUNTED from `post-sitemap.xml`: **933 posts** (2011→2026;
  sitemap lastmod spans 2014-06→2026-08-04). Category breakdown (counted):
  anime-articles 368 · japan-culture 196 · otaku-culture 79 · folklore-and-urban-legends 61 ·
  history 50 · book-reviews 50 · writing 39 · finds-and-ramblings 35 · philosophy 26 ·
  video-games 13 · misc 15. Realistic per-title critique inventory: the ~368 anime-articles
  plus part of otaku-culture — but many are trend/culture essays, so plausibly **150–250
  titles** get a dedicated essay (ESTIMATE).
- **robots.txt + TOS posture:** **Explicitly refuses AI-agent access.** robots.txt (fetched
  in full, key lines): `Content-Signal: search=yes,ai-train=no,use=reference` (EU
  2019/790-style reservation) · `Disallow: /` blocks for **ClaudeBot**, GPTBot, CCBot,
  Amazonbot, Google-Extended, Applebot-Extended, Bytespider, meta-externalagent ·
  `User-agent: * → Allow: /` · `Sitemap: https://www.japanpowered.com/sitemap_index.xml`.
  Enforced in practice: WebFetch (AI-identified) got **HTTP 403** on every page while
  robots.txt itself served; a plain browser UA gets 200 — i.e. a WAF specifically walls out
  AI fetchers. No formal TOS page found; the About page is warmly permissive to *humans*
  ("You are more than welcome to use JP's articles to help you with college or school
  assignments"), and `use=reference` signals reference-with-attribution is acceptable —
  but **automated ingestion is off the table**. Scouting disclosure: this dossier used a
  handful of browser-UA fetches (homepage, about, sitemaps, one essay) to characterize the
  site; no review parsing, nothing stored beyond this dossier.
- **Accessibility:** Technically excellent if access were permitted — full WP sitemaps,
  clean category slugs, RSS feed exists (also 403 to AI agents). For the pilot this is moot:
  **manual-path only** (same bucket as Sakuga Blog / Anime Feminist): owner/human reading,
  structured claims + ≤40-word attributed quotes, or a future permission arrangement —
  Kincaid is a named, reachable author with a stated pro-information-access ethos, so an
  email asking permission is unusually likely to get an answer.
- **Lens/voice:** Cultural-analysis-heavy: anime read through folklore, history, psychology,
  philosophy, and library-science sensibilities; measured, citation-backed, unhurried.
  Closer to "anime as cultural text" than "anime as craft object." For Kuro this is a
  **legacy/context/characters** source, not a visuals/pacing source.
- **Proposed trust tier:** **C** — single real-name author, 15-year active archive, argued
  and cited essays, but moderate per-title verdict density, craft axes mostly absent, and
  manual-only access caps volume. (A case exists for B on the legacy/cultural axis
  specifically — owner's call.)
